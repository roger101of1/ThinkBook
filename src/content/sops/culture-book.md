## Purpose
The Culture Book is how Thinkspace defines who we are: our core purpose, our core values, the founder's expectations, how we treat each other (co-commitment, zero gossip, feedback), what promotion looks like, what "no longer a fit" looks like, and your 90-day onboarding schedule. Read it on Day 1 and come back to it often.

## Core purpose

**We strengthen the social fabric around entrepreneurs and contribute to their success.**

We believe the greatest workplaces are built the same way athletes train: through discipline, consistency, mutual trust, feedback, and the willingness to grow together.

## Ethos, mindset and principles: a co-committed workplace

**Co-Commitment** means we each take **100% responsibility** for our own experience, growth, and impact. We support each other's creativity and success without controlling, rescuing, blaming, or seeking approval. We choose co-commitment because **we are building a race crew, not a rescue crew.**

In a co-committed workplace:
1. We speak authentically, even when it is hard.
2. We listen generously, with curiosity over judgment.
3. We encourage each other's fullest potential without sacrificing our own integrity.
4. We are responsible **to** each other, not **for** each other.

### What entanglements are

Entanglements happen when unconscious patterns (control, blame, guilt, approval-seeking, rescuing) replace clear agreements and open communication. They erode trust and make work about personalities and approval instead of excellence and shared purpose.

You might hear:

| Pattern | What it sounds like |
|---|---|
| Control | "If they change, then I can be okay." |
| Blame | "They make me feel this way." |
| Guilt | "I feel bad if I succeed and they don't." |
| Approval-seeking | "I can't say that, I might upset them." |
| Rescuing | "I have to fix this for them or they'll fail." |

### Gossip (entanglement) vs co-commitment (high trust)

| | Gossip | Co-Commitment |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | Talking *about* someone instead of *to* them | Speaking directly and respectfully to the person involved |
| **Intention** | Seek approval, vent, avoid discomfort | Solve problems openly, strengthen trust |
| **Energy** | Blame, resentment, division | Ownership, curiosity, unity |
| **Peer to peer** | "Can you believe what they did?" (to a third party) | "Hey, can we talk? I noticed something on my mind." |
| **Leadership** | "I don't know why leadership made that decision. It's stupid." (to coworkers) | "I'd like to understand the decision. Could we talk about it?" (to leadership) |
| **Escalation** | Whispering concerns to coworkers | Requesting a one-on-one with the person or leader directly |
| **Undermining** | "The managers are out of touch. Not worth trying." | "I noticed a gap between what's happening and what's expected. Can we bridge it?" |
| **Impact** | Weakens trust, silos, fear | Builds trust, ownership, resilience |

### What to do if anyone is gossiping (three steps)

1. **Name it:** "Sounds like gossip."
2. **Shut it down:** "Let's stick to our agreement: direct conversation."
3. **Offer help:** "How can I support you in bringing it up to them?"

> **Warning:** Violating the Zero Gossip policy is one of the "no longer a fit" standards below.

### Our framework for feedback: RLFH (Reinforcement Learning from Humans)

1. **Consent.** Ask: "Can I provide you with some feedback?"
2. **Method.** Ask: "How would you best like to receive the feedback?" and offer three modes:
   - **Coach:** you have the inner wisdom; I will ask questions to draw it out.
   - **Mentor:** I will share experiences from similar situations; take what resonates.
   - **Teacher:** here are the steps: 1, 2, 3.
3. **Another way** is to ask the person to put on their beginner's-mindset hat.

## When we know someone is ready for promotion

- Mastery within their role.
- Consistently meets and exceeds expectations; an A player.
- Great cultural fit.
- Can train others.
- Leadership qualities.
- Takes ownership of their own growth.
- Studies the job description they want and actively learns the skills.
- Asks their manager: "I want specific growth in (Leadership, Operations, Sales). What are the ways I can get that?" or "What's the biggest challenge you're working on and how can I help?"

## When we know someone is no longer a fit: non-negotiable standards

| Area | Standards |
|---|---|
| **Work performance and responsibility** | Failing to meet clearly defined goals. Leaving your post unattended without approval beyond scheduled breaks/lunch. Failing to notify the team when taking unexpected time off or leaving early. Repeated lateness, absenteeism, or abuse of PTO. |
| **Trust and integrity** | Losing company property (keys, equipment) through negligence. Lying about work completed. Stealing from the company. |
| **Culture and team fit** | Refusing to participate in Daily Huddles, Skills and Levels training, and operational improvements. Violating the Zero Gossip policy. Being technically strong but unwilling to collaborate. Poor customer service that damages the brand. |
| **Refusing to work on-site** | We are a place-based company; our members are inside our spaces. **Working physically from our spaces is required, not optional.** Remote work is limited and must be approved. |

**Our philosophy:** We trade value for value: pay, benefits, community and opportunity for your time, energy, and best effort.

## 90-day onboarding schedule

| Step | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Welcome Meeting** | Your kick-off. |
| **Weekly 30-minute one-on-ones** | Essential for giving and receiving feedback. This is *your* meeting, to focus on what is most important to you. |
| **Review Training Schedule** | Walk through the training plan (this Trello board). |
| **30/60/90-day review** | Recaps the Expectation Meeting. |
| **6-month review** | Recaps the Expectation Meeting again. |

**Sign-off:** "I understand the expectations we have reviewed and that the only way I can achieve my goals is to first achieve these expectations." (Employee and Manager signatures and dates.)
