# WELCOME

Welcome to Thinkspace (“Thinkspace”). We want you to know you are an important member of our team. No, you are a critical member of our team and I want you to know that we may just care more about you than anywhere else you have ever worked. We appreciate having you join us. Welcome to the Thinkspace team\!

### What Entanglements Are

Entanglements happen when unconscious patterns — control, blame, guilt, approval-seeking, rescuing — replace clear agreements and open communication.

In an entangled workplace, people get stuck in drama, hidden resentments, or caretaking others at the expense of themselves and the company.

Entanglements erode trust. They make work about personalities and approval instead of about excellence, collaboration, and shared purpose.

In an entangled workplace, you might hear or feel:

“If they change, then I can be okay.” (Control)  
 – or – “They just need to listen to me and everything would be better.”  

“They make me feel this way.” (Blame)  
 – or – “I can’t do my best because of how they act.”  

“I feel bad if I succeed and they don’t — maybe I should hold back.” (Guilt)  
 – or – “It feels selfish to want to stand out when others are struggling.”  

“I can’t say that, I might upset them.” (Approval-seeking)  
 – or – “I need them to like me, even if it means staying silent.”  

“I have to fix this for them or they’ll fail.” (Rescuing)  
 – or – “If I don’t jump in, everything will fall apart.”

## EQUAL EMPLOYMENT POLICY

Thinkspace is an equal opportunity employer. Thinkspace does not discriminate based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, age, disability, marital status, sexual orientation, or any other legally protected status.

Our policy of non-discrimination applies to all terms and conditions of employment, including but not limited to hiring, placement, promotion, termination, reduction in force, transfer, leaves of absence, compensation and training. We comply with all applicable federal, state and local laws that prohibit discrimination in employment.

While managers and supervisors are charged with the responsibility of preventing discrimination, the success of our equal opportunity efforts depends on the unbiased cooperative attitudes and respectful actions of all employees.

## NON-DISCRIMINATION AND ANTI-HARASSMENT POLICY

All employees have the right to work in a non-discriminatory and non-intimidating environment. Harassment of any employee on the basis of race, religion, color, sex, age, marital status, sexual orientation and/or the presence of any physical, mental or sensory disability can be a form of discrimination and a serious violation of Company policy. DISCRIMINATION AND HARASSMENT WILL NOT BE CONDONED OR TOLERATE BY THINKSPACE.

Harassment can take many forms and can include slurs, comments, jokes, innuendoes, unwelcome compliments, cartoons, visual displays, e-mails, prank, and/or other verbal, visual or physical conduct relating to an individual which:

Has the purpose or effect of creating an intimidating, hostile or offensive working environment

Has the purpose or effect of unreasonably interfering with an individual’s work performance

Unreasonably affects an individual’s employment opportunities.

Sexual Harassment is harassment that occurs when the types of verbal, visual and/or physical conduct described above are sexual in nature or are gender-based, i.e., directed at a person because of his/her gender. Sexual harassment exists in the workplace when:

Submission to the conduct is, either explicitly or implicitly, a term or condition of employment

Submission to or rejection of the conduct is used as the basis of any employment decision affecting such individual and/or

The conduct unreasonably interferes with the individual’s job performance or creates a work environment that is intimidating, hostile or offensive.

Like other forms of harassment, SEXUAL HARASSMENT WILL NOT BE CONDONED OR TOLERATED BY THINKSPACE.

  
Non-Discrimination. All Thinkspace employees, whatever their title or status, are prohibited from discriminating against or harassing any other employee and/or from retaliating, in any way, against anyone who makes a good faith compliant of discrimination or harassment. Any associate who is found to have violated this policy will be subject to discipline up to and including termination of employment.

Thinkspace takes its non-discrimination and anti-harassment policies very seriously. Associates have a duty to report any discrimination or harassment-related problems that arise with Thinkspace managers or employees, and others whom an employee may encounter during work to the Human Resources Department. Any complaint received will be promptly investigated and corrective action taken if appropriate.

Thinkspace prohibits employment-based retaliation against anyone who brings a complaint of sexual harassment or discrimination or who speaks as a witness in the investigation of a complaint of sexual harassment.

## ALCOHOL AND DRUG POLICY

Employees are prohibited from possessing, using, or being under the influence of alcohol while on the job or on company property. Employees are also prohibited from possessing, using or trafficking in illegally used drugs while on the job or on company property.

Employees who are under the influence of alcohol or illegally used drugs while on Company property or while representing Thinkspace will be subject to disciplinary action, up to and including discharge. The use, sale or possession of alcohol or illegally used drugs while on Thinkspace’s time or property will also subject an employee to disciplinary action, up to and including termination. The determination that this policy has been violated, as well as the implementation and enforcement of this policy, shall be within the discretion of the company.

Pre-Employment Testing. Thinkspace may require potential new employees to submit to a pre-employment chemical substance/drug screening. This procedure consists of obtaining a urine specimen from the individual and sending it to an authorized drug testing laboratory. The results of the testing must be returned to the Human Resources Department with satisfactory results before the potential employee may begin work.

Post-Employment Testing. When an employee’s manager or supervisor has reason to suspect that the employee is under the influence of alcohol or other chemical substance, he/she may initiate specific screening of the employee to determine whether the employee is “under the influence.”

The administrator or supervisor may order testing when he/she has “reasonable suspicion” that the employee is under the influence of alcohol or drugs. For the purposes of this policy, “reasonable suspicion” may be based on evidence of unusual behavior on the job, which might include a decrease in productivity, abnormally high accident rates, repeated tardiness or absence from work, aggressive or violent behavior, emotional outbursts or moodiness, sensory or motor skill malfunctions, or exhibiting behavior that poses a threat to self or others.

If the employee is observed to be either physically or mentally impaired, the manager or supervisor will initiate the testing process, and then arrange for someone to take the employee home.

If the employee refuses to consent to a chemical substance/drug test, the employee’s refusal will be noted. Refusal to consent to the screening test is a violation of office policy and may result in disciplinary action, including termination of employment.

Random Testing. Periodically, Thinkspace may require the random testing of employees to ensure that employees are not performing their duties under the influence of alcohol/drugs. This testing does not require the presumption of “reasonable suspicion” and is performed at random so that all employees may, on occasion, be called upon to provide specimens for testing.

In those instances where any employee refuses to consent to a random chemical substance/drug test, the employee’s refusal will be noted. Refusal to consent to the random screening is a violation of office policy and may result in disciplinary action, including termination of employment.

## INTRODUCTORY PERIOD

The first 90 days of employment of Thinkspace are considered an introductory period. During this time, each manager will be evaluating each new employee’s performance and ability to meet Company standards. ## EMPLOYMENT OF RELATIVES

Thinkspace does not discriminate in employment decisions or policies in violation of law on the basis of marital status. Employment of qualified spouses, domestic living partners and relatives of current associates is permitted unless it is concluded that the employment would place one or both of the relatives in a situation of actual or reasonably foreseeable conflict between their interests and the Company’s, such as if one spouse would have the authority or practical power to supervise, appoint, remove, or discipline the other, or one spouse would be responsible for auditing the work of the other. “Relatives” are spouses, children, parents, siblings, grandparents, grandchildren, aunts, uncles, first cousins and corresponding in-laws of “step” relations.

This policy also applies to employees who marry while employed by the Company. No employee will be discharged due to a conflict of interest because he or she has married another employee unless the conflict cannot be resolved by transferring one or both employees.

The Company prohibits employment of relatives and domestic living partners of policy level officers of significant customers, competitors, regulatory agencies and others with whom we deal, in order to avoid the reality or appearance of improper influence or favor, and to protect our confidential information.

## CATEGORIES OF EMPLOYMENT

Thinkspace employees are classified as contract, part-time or full-time based on the conditions of employment and hours worked. Your classification partly determines the type of benefits, if any you will receive.

Contract Employees. Contract employees qualify as independent contractors and work at a set hourly or project rate. Thinkspace does not guarantee availability of hours.

Contract employees will not be entitled to leaves of absence, PTO, or group insurance.

Part-time Employees. Part-time employees are defined as employees who work less than 32 hours week and have a set employment schedule at Thinkspace.

Regular part-time employees are not eligible for PTO and employee benefits unless otherwise provided in the relevant benefit plan document or within their employment contract.

#### Full-time Employees. Regular full-time employees are both non-exempt employees who are regularly scheduled to work at least 32 hours per week and salaried exempt employees. In general, regular full-time employees are eligible for the benefits outlined in this handbook, subject to other eligibility rules described in our benefits policies and plans.

Exempt and nonexempt refers to employee’s status under the Fair and Labor Standards Act and corresponding state statutes governing wage and hour law in the United States. In general, qualified executive, administrative or professional employees are classified as exempt employees. Salaried “exempt” employees are paid on a salary basis and are exempt from receiving pay for overtime work. “Nonexempt” employees are paid on hourly wage for actual hours worked, and receive pay for overtime work. If you have a question about the status of your position, please check with your manager or the Human Resources Department.

## PERFORMANCE REVIEWS

While Thinkspace believes in continual, informal communication, a formal review of an employee’s performance is also important. It is Company policy to conduct an annual performance review on a schedule determined by the Human Resources Department with all full-time employees at least once a year, at the same time as comparable positions or all employees at a given location are reviewed. The appraisal is intended to be a development activity to assist and motivate employees to attain their maximum potential. Additional performance reviews may be performed throughout the year.

In the case of promotion to a new position, employees will receive a performance review on or around the 90th day of employment in a new position.

## PAY PERIODS

Thinkspace has established a bi-weekly pay period. Paychecks are issued every fifth and twentieth of the month. Thinkspace requires all non-exempt employees to enter their actual hours worked on the company time clock.

## TIME KEEPING REQUIREMENTS

All Thinkspace nonexempt employees are required to keep track of their hours through the company time clock system. The hours must accurately reflect exact time worked. Time clock updates are due to the Human Resources Department before 8:00 a.m. on the Monday before payday. Employees are required to complete their own time clock entries on a “real time” (neither in advance or delayed) basis. Completing or entering time clock information for another associate or falsifying hours worked are forbidden and will lead to disciplinary action up to and including termination.

## OVERTIME FOR NONEXEMPT EMPLOYEES

Overtime work for nonexempt employees, when requested by management, is usually on a voluntary basis. Efforts will be made to keep overtime to a minimum with careful planning by management. There may be times when overtime is mandatory and nonexempt employees are expected to comply with a requirement to work overtime.

In the event that overtime is worked, nonexempt employees will receive overtime compensation in accordance with state and federal laws. All overtime work must have prior approval from management. Paid Time Off (PTO) hours are not treated as hours worked in the calculation of overtime.

## PAYROLL DEDUCTIONS

## FAMILY AND MEDICAL LEAVE OF ABSENCE

Due to the size of ThinkSpace, the company qualifies for WA Paid Family and Medical Leave.

Eligibility. To be eligible for WA PFML, an employee must have been employed and has worked at least 820 hours.

Amount and Nature of Leave. Under WA PFML, eligible employees are permitted to take up to 12 work weeks of paid leave. Paid leave is fully distributed by the State of Washington.

Reasons for Taking Leave. Unpaid WA PFML, leave is granted for any of the following reasons:

To care for your child after birth or placement for adoption or foster care

To care for a family member who has a serious health condition

For a serious health condition that makes you unable to perform the essential functions of your job.

Definition of Serious Health Condition. “Serious health condition” when used in this policy means an illness or condition that involves: inpatient care; a period of incapacity of more than three consecutive calendar days that also involves multiple treatment by or a regimen of continuing treatment prescribed by, a health care provider; any incapacity due to pregnancy or prenatal care; a chronic condition which requires periodic treatment and may cause episodic periods of incapacity (e.g., diabetes, epilepsy, asthma, etc.). A period of incapacity which is permanent/long term condition requiring continuing supervision of a health care provider; a period of absence due to multiple treatments by a healthcare provider either for restorative surgery after an accident or injury or for a condition which would likely result in an incapacity of more than three consecutive calendar days in the absence of medical intervention or treatment.

“Incapacity” when used in this policy means inability to work, attend school, or perform other regular daily activities.

Applying for Leave. An employee must provide Thinkspace with at least 30 days notice before family and medical leave is to begin if the need for leave is foreseeable based on an expected birth, placement for adoption or foster care, or planned medical treatment for a serious health condition of the employee or of a family member. If 30 days notice is not practical, due to a lack of knowledge of approximately when leave will be required to begin, a change in circumstances, or a medical emergency, notice must be given as soon as possible and practical given the employee’s individual circumstances. The notice must specify the purpose and anticipated dates of leave.

Documentation. Thinkspace requires a medical certification from a health care provider to support family and medical leave requests because of a serious health condition of the employee or of a family member. The certification must be on the form furnished by Thinkspace (contact the Human Resources Department for a Leave Request Form). For a foreseeable FMLA leave, a fully-completed medical certificate statement must be submitted no later than 15 days prior to the commencement date of leave. For emergency leave requests, a fully completed medial certification statement must be submitted within 15 days of the employee’s request for leave.

Failure to provide timely notice when a need for family and medical leave is foreseeable or to provide in a timely manner a requested medial certification to substantiate the need for family and medical leave, can result in delay or denial of family and medical leave.

Intermittent Leave. Under some circumstances, leave may be taken intermittently, which means taking leave in shorter blocks of times, or by reducing your normal weekly or daily work schedule. WA FPML leave may be taken intermittently if medically necessary for a serious health condition. If WA FPML leave is for birth or placement for adoption or foster care, use of intermittent leave is subject to Thinkspace’s approval.

Employer Designated Leave. Thinkspace reserves the right to designate any absence due to a serious health condition as WA PFML leave. The employee will receive written notice of any such designation.

Reinstatement Rights.

When a family and medical leave is occasioned by a serious health condition rendering the employment unable to perform his/her job, Thinkspace may require, as a condition to reinstatement, medical certification from a health care provider that the employee is able to resume work. The certification form is available from the Human Resources Department and must be returned to Thinkspace prior to the anticipated date of return to work.

The following conduct can result in delay/denial of reinstatement: failure to provide a requested medical or fitness for duty certification; failure to return to work on the designated return to date; or fraudulent obtainment of leave.

Failure to return to work at the end of the leave period will be considered a resignation, unless an extension is applied for in writing and granted prior to the completion of the initial leave. Requests for extension must be supported by adequate medical certification.

Status Reports and Recertification. Employees on extended WA PFML leave are required to furnish Thinkspace with periodic reports concerning their status and intent to return to work. Additionally, subsequent recertification concerning an employee’s medical condition and continued necessity of leave may also be required.

Health Insurance Benefits During Leave. If you are covered by Thinkspace’s insurance plan, Thinkspace will pay its portion of your insurance coverage through the end of the month in which you begin your leave. After that you must pay your entire insurance premiums, at the group rate, to continue your coverage for the remainder of your leave. If you are on a concurrent family and medical leave, Thinkspace will pay its portion of your health coverage during this time.

Other Insurance and Benefits, such as life insurance will continue on the same basis as during regular employment during any portion of the FMLA leave that is paid (i.e., while the employee is exhausting any accrued Paid Time Off hours). If FMLA leave is unpaid, the employee will be responsible during the leave for the full cost of the insurance premium(s).

Applicable Law. Thinkspace complies with applicable state and federal laws which may supersede this policy in certain cases.
